Critical

A lattice of spins. Each one points up or down, and each one wants to agree with its neighbors. There's no external field, no central controller, no instruction that says "align." The only energy is the interaction between adjacent spins — same direction, low energy; opposite, high. The only noise is heat.

Below a critical temperature Tc, something happens. The spins spontaneously organize. The system magnetizes — not because anyone told it to, but because local agreement, at low enough temperature, cascades into global order. Above Tc, thermal noise wins, and the lattice is disordered. The transition is sharp. Lars Onsager solved this exactly in 1944 — one of the few nontrivial phase transitions with an exact solution. Tc = 2/ln(1+√2) ≈ 2.269.

Every spin only talks to its four nearest neighbors. Yet the whole lattice decides together. Drag the temperature past Tc and watch order appear or vanish. Add an external field and watch the symmetry break. Click to flip individual spins and watch the neighborhood respond.

This is the paradigmatic answer to the Forge question: where does order come from when nobody's in charge? Not from a controller. Not from a blueprint. From local interactions at the right temperature — order is what happens when cooperation is cheap enough and noise is low enough that agreement becomes the path of least resistance.
